Required Skills

This kit is for experienced builders who have mechanical, soldering, know how find PCB etch, through hole issues and repair them, trace through circuit diagrams to troubleshoot problems to device level, work with Teflon Coax preparation and have RPI administration experience.

If you need help there are multiple folks in B26 group that can assist you or build your unit.

SAFETY WARNINGS

  • There is 240VAC on the front panel switch, and if the press on connectors come off you have loose 240VAC where it can shock or kill you. Best Practice cut off connectors, add 6” extension soldered directly to switch, and heat shrink to insure no accidental contact with 240VAC.
  • When working on the amp always unplug the 240VAC power cable, and ensure chassis is attached to a good earth ground.
  • LDMOS and IC’s are static sensitive. Use anti-static grounding strap on your wrist or leg connected to earth ground.
  • During high power tuning both tuner and LPF will have at least 350V and miss tunes 4-5 times that when out of resonance (high swr). And when tuned antenna, its feed lines, baluns, need to have 3KW rating minimum.
  • RF burns are possible with any linear amp.
  • Do not just wire power plug and turn power on, the amp is not complete, has parts in between the display and control board.
  • Always have 50ohm 2500W dummy load attached to Ant 1 when building, calibrating or working on amp. These amps need a load.
  • Test your antenna, grounding, feed lines, balun’s with your radio to insure they have low SWR and are functioning properly before connecting your newly finished amp.
  • LDMOS parts are very sensitive to gate voltage, use only DMM manually set on 10Meg range to ensure your meter does not blow the parts up. -6 to +11 is maximum voltage LDMOS can survive. Do not use traditional mechanical meters.
  • The number one cause of blowing up LDMOS is OVERDRIVING. 1-2W without an attenuator drives them to full output, a 13db attenuator maximum drive is 30W CW or SSB. Most radios surge over 120W on initial key then after some time drop to 100W when power setting is 100W. This may blow up your LDMOS first time it happens. Consider a 16DB or greater Attenuator which allows about 55-60W maximum drive. The protection circuit will shut down, which should save the LDMOS however make sure your rig has maximum power set on every band below 30W with 13db or 60W with 16db.

Shutdown Procedure

  1. Put amp in Standby Mode, and make sure radio is not keying amp.
  2. Using VNC or external wireless mouse/keyboard hit ALT-F4, this closes pa055 application and drops you on the RPI desktop.
  3. In the lower left corner locate the raspberry icon, click and bottom selection is shutdown, select it then it will popup choices.
  4. Select the shutdown button, hit OK and wait for screen to go black, both Mega and RPI3 are now shutdown.
  5. Now turn off front panel power. When you power up the amp will automatically return to the pa055 screen above.

Calibration Steps

Step 1 – Temporarily Disable Input Protection

  • Turn R1 CCW until it clicks. In step 13 we adjust this.

Step 2 – Temporarily Disable LPF Forward Power Protection

  • Turn R17 CCW until it clicks. In step 12 we adjust this.

  • Turn SPARE CCW until it clicks then CW 7.5 turns to center it.

  • With DMM, measure actual voltage which should be 53.6 +/- .25VDC.
  • Look at display see 53.6 V adjust R22 to be 53.6V.
  • Careful 50V at 50+ amps here don’t short things out.
  • Display below is correctly adjusted for 53.6V

Step 5 – Temporarily Remove Bias on The PA (RF2K5 Pallet)

  • Locate the two bias 3 pin jumps on the top of the PA towards the front. From factory it will look like this.
  • Move both jumps to rear position like top one in this picture.
  • These grounds both LDMOS devices gates protecting them during Calibration.
  • These will be put in the forward position (towards front) in Step 15.
  • Note PA input cable is disconnected from the Relay board not shown is this photo.

Step 6 – Test RF Coax Wiring

This test verifies the RF coax connections. It bypasses the PA, but tests proper wiring of the Antenna Tuner bypass relays and relay board.

  • Connect your Transceiver to the RF2K+ TRX PL-293 connector on rear panel.
  • 2500W dummy load to ANT 1
  • PTT Linear Key line from your Transceiver to KEY (RCA) jack
  • Note: USB port is connected to USB powered hub to allow wireless keyboard and mouse if not using VNC.
  • Shutdown RPI3 using the correct shutdown procedure
  • Turn of power switch on front panel so amp is OFF.
  • You need two watt/SWR meters, Transceiver one is OK is it has both PWR and SWR, one on dummy load must be at least 2KW.
  • Make sure your cabling is 50 ohm and similar L/C and velocity factor or you will get false SWR readings in upper bands.
  • Set Transceiver in 7.2Mhz, CW mode, 50W power and compare to SWR/Watt meter on output should have similar readings.
  • Transceiver should see near 1:1, try all bands make a chart of SWR input and output.
  • The chart should be near 1:1 for all bands if not investigate why Tuner or Teflon coax or Relay board is creating high SWR at specific frequency bands.
  • See troubleshooting section if need in the Appendix.
  • This is a critical step and must pass before moving forward.

Step 7 - Calibrate the Controller Frequency Detector

This is critical step, calibration is needed to insure, the Arduino/Controller reads 8:1 divided frequency to determine the correct LPF and Tuner relays to select for each frequency band.

This amp changes bands based on this (UNIVERSAL MODE) or CAT, or another interface.

You cannot change band from amp front panel touch screen.

UNIVERSAL mode is the default if CAT or other mode fails to select correct band.

The small coax from the X9/X99 on the Relay board to X1 connector on Controller board provides a small sample

of the input RF to the Controller to determine frequency.

  • Turn on the amp.

Step 13 – Calibrate Input Power Protection

Even though we have a attenuator, it is critical to insure this adjustment is correct.

The RF2K+ incorporates TVS diodes to protect the gates of the LDMOS devices. The TVS diode will help protect gates for short duration until the Controller senses the overdrive condition, and shuts down.

A RF detector circuit on the Relay board, produces a voltage proportional to the input power and the Controller has fast hardware circuit to disable the input.

When the input protection triggers, an Input Power Too High! Press Reset message followed by Current Limiting PA Module message is displayed and the TX relay are released removing drive and bias from the RF2K5 pallet.

To clear the error and enable the amp again, Select the small GREEN reset button on screen to clear the error.

Note you will be purposely tripping this to set the protection up as the PA is out of the circuit no damage will occur.

The critical part is setting Transceiver power maximum output to not exceed power that matches the attenuator installed on the amp.

If you do the LDMOS may be blown. Be careful new Transceivers jump up to 140+W PEP for few microseconds before stabilizing at 100W.

You must take this in to account while determining your trip point setting.

Remember LDMOS produces full output at 1-2W input which is why correct Transceiver power setting to match the attenuator is so important.

RF2K+ Test Plan

This test plan is Optional however it will characterize your amp on dummy load all bands and modes, then in 2nd pass on live antennas with On-Air tests.

This validates your build from an Operational perspective at the same time trains you on amp Operations.

A test plan creates a series of steps to prepare for a specific test on the Unit Under Test (UUT), then the test steps are executed and compared to an expected result. If measured = expected then a specific test step is PASSED, if not, the entire test series FAILS, if PASSED then next specific test step is evaluated and so forth until all specific tests in the unit test are completed for the function in the UUT under evaluation. If ALL specific test steps PASS then Unit Test PASSES, and specific test step FAILURE fails the Unit and total System TEST.

The main body containing all the tests is the System Test, with in the System Test are Unit tests for specific functions, then individual specific test steps within the Unit tests.

After UUT PASSES System Test, then Integration Test of complete end to end Solution in this case ON AIR test is evaluated.

IF ALL TEST PASS Including Integration Tests, then UUT is released to Production Ready status and can be immediately used in active operations as the UUT is now fully characterized and tested.

Again, this is Optional, in Ham Radio service, IACS it is not required. In commercial Integration Test is required to enable Integrator and manufacture to be paid for the AS Built and Integration Tested Solution.

Does testing help the ham, yes if you’re interested in checking that your UUT is fully functional and works with your station configuration.

Test plans also document actual UUT viability. It provides a complete set of documentation AS Built with test results which become the baseline for the lifecycle of the solution and the reference point for rapid assessment of faults and recovery to operational status.

Test plans frequently are used to certify and meet local communications standards.

You will need to purchase a raspberry PI 4 with 4 or 8GB and at least a 32GB SD card. Buy premium name brand SD cards, cheap clones are known to cause problems (SanDisk is good quality unit).

Troubleshooting

RPI3 does not boot

Ah the dreaded black screen of I’m not coming up. Most of the time it’s just loose power connection to the RPI, occasionally, is a corrupted SD boot card. If it’s just power there will be no lights on the RPI board, if RPI has power red and green LED’s will be blinking, if they are lit and stay on, and the screen has not shown color test pattern, the 4 raspberry icons, and long list of text scrolling down the screen, boot SD is corrupt.

Improper shutdown of the RPI can damage SD boot and the Arduino Mega sketch load.

Another issue for RPI boot failure is running out of partition space on 8GB SD cards, after a time, it simply fills up with logs and files then wont reboot.

If you did your SD backup during the build, swap out backup boot on it.

  1. Hit ALT-F4 t get to desktop
  2. Open Terminal icon on lower left of screen, this opens a white on black terminal window to work from.
  3. At the $ prompt, type raspi-config, this will start the configurator.
  4. Tab down to Advanced, in Advanced select A1 Expand filesystem, finish, yes to reboot.
  5. Now on reboot, it will expand from 7.9GB to 29+GB making all the space available on your booted SD.
  6. On RPI3 power up on the new SD image.
  7. When booted you will be back on line with 29GB file system.

Odd issues

PCB issues on some builds during incoming inspection, Control and SWR boards have been bad and the Rf sensor small coax from relay to Control board was found to be bad.

High transceiver SWR

When you get to setting the input protection, hit OPERATE (GREEN) and transceiver show high SWR. R1 CCW until it clicks and stop now turn CW 7 turns near center of pot. Control board sense errors

If the small coax on the Control board to Relay board run is shorted or open, Control board will not pass input or later steps.

Antenna switch or T/R problems

On some relay boards RJ45 cable is not fully seated and this can cause K2 or K3 to get out of sync causing high transceiver SWR.

PCB issues

Several boards have been found with corrosion, black feed thru’ s, dirty, solder balls shorting adjacent traces, cold solder joint, open feed thru’ s, and over etched open traces under solder mask.

New boards should have shiny not grainy solder joints, feed thru’ s should be zero ohms between both sides of the PCB. Retouch with solder, clean with PCB cleaner.

RPI update or upgrade fails

The common failures are:

  • Files not available or server is too busy and fails to download
  • RPI is too far behind for normal upgrade –fix-missing to work
  • On original 8GB SD cards you might run out of space, due to temp, log and other files collecting over time, then any update or upgrade runs out of space. See how to use SD Copy from 8GB to 32GB SD and expand file system which solves this common problem.

When running RPI or Linux upgrades occasionally the downloads get busy and fail.

Usually later it will run until complete. 

Another reason is the apt upgrade command fails as it was not able to load all the files needed or RPI OS base content is too far behind to work.   (Very rare event in commercial data centers where these two commands are run daily keeping the RPI or other Linux server for the purpose of keeping servers always on latest patches, security bits and so forth.) But expected in B26 initial update/upgrade operations.

This upgrade failure does not cause any harm, as upgrade confirms it has all the files update recommended, but normal upgrade –fix-missing downloads much less than full release which is all files downloaded and checksum verified, before it starts any installation.  If there is too much difference between current base and latest upgrade, it quits and complains and does not make any changes to the current system.

What apt update does is download the list of new available files at that point in time that can be upgraded.  

Apt-get is slightly different way to use update and upgrade.  Both formats work fine. Apt is more common command.

How do you fix a failed upgrade –fix-missing? Run apt dist-upgrade -y.

The apt dist-upgrade -y worked because it over wrote your whole RPI stretch distribution release, which is actually how you fix a failed normal apt upgrade --fix-missing, if –fix-missing did not automatically work with normal upgrade command.  And –fix-missing flag is redundant, not needed with dist-upgrade -y and ignored.

How much difference between dist and normal upgrade, depends on how far out of date your current release is on your RPI.

With dist a lot more files are downloaded up to 3GB, while upgrade –fix-missing only downloads and installs just the new files.

Most of the B26 RPI3’s has, never been through a Linux Raspbian upgrade, the normal apt upgrade which downloads just the latest files fails because the RPI was on very old copies of stretch release. If you just installed 055.250 and did the RFK Menu->Upgrade procedure, apt upgrade –fix-missing will work. (At least until 287 gets too far behind to work, then use apt dist-upgrade -y to solve the problem understanding lot longer amount of time to upgrade RPI.)

B26 RF2K+ Mechanical Assembly and Disassembly

If you need to service PSU’s or cabling in the chassis disassembly is needed.

  • Shutdown using correct procedure
  • Unplug power cord.
  • Remove 4 rear fan screws, and 2 screws this will release the rear panel.
  • Unplug PA SMA and ribbon cable.
  • Unsolder PA OUT white Teflon coax
  • Tilt the PA top outwards then will slide past screws and plastic cable clips.
  • There are three screws in the PA side of the center divider
  • Unplug the DB25 cable from TUNER
  • Start removing the 2 attaching the TUNER tray to the center divider
  • Remove the 2 flush screws on other side of TUNER tray
  • Remove the TUNER and fold outward taking care not to damage white Teflon cables
  • Remove LPF front ribbon cable
  • Remove the single screw holding the LPF tray to the center divider
  • Remove 2 flush screws on other side of LPF tray
  • Remove the LPF and fold outward taking care not to damage white Teflon cables
  • Remove remaining screws holding the divider there are two behind the Control board use a forceps to hold nut and Phillips screw driver on other side to remove top and bottom screw and nut.
  • Remove the center divider.
  • While your there, remove the 4 flush screws from the front panel, the Power switch slide on connectors, and fold the display on its face so you can work cables easily.
  • Cut off the power switch slide on connectors, add 6” of same gage wire to the existing power switch wiring, apply heat shrink tubing on that extension, slide 2 additional heat shrink tubing pieces, solder the switch directly to switch, slide heat shrink over connections, shrink the heat shrink tubing. This removes a very dangerous shock hazard that can occur if the original slide on connectors, slip off when you have the front panel tilted out and power on to work on amp. With the extension you have enough length to lay front panel flat, and the power switch is completely insulated, lowering significantly the shock hazard.

Now is an excellent time to visually inspect cables, solder joints, feed throughs, cable routing.

Some PCB’s have etching issues, open plate throughs, solder balls causing intermittent problems.

In most cases after cleaning with PCB cleaner, or laquer thinner, you can use exacto knife to carefully trim etch issue if a short, if open, use exacto knife to scrap 2mm of solder mask off on each side, then cut short wire 26Ga and solder across the open etch. Open feed throughs are hard to find, but any feed through that is not shiny, does not have solder in it or looks black is highly suspect. You can in some cases use DMM to ohm from both sides of the board, but generally is faster just to take 26Ga wire in solder it thru the feed thru, soldering both sides.

The other thing is cold solder joints, any joint that is not shiny is a problem. You need to reflow with tin/lead 60/40 solder, and little flux to clean up the solder joint.

Solder balls, old etchant, flux residue, and corrosion need cleaning. PCB cleaner, followed by cotton ball and q-tip dipped in laquer thinner is used, and keep scrubbing until the problems are completely clean.

On connectors, DeOxIt drops or D5 spray used sparingly, will keep them in good condition for long time.

With a mix of digital and very high current RF/PS current flowing through cables, and PCB’s it is very important to take the time to do this clean up before powering the amp up. If you have amp running it is still a good idea to spend the 1-2hrs it takes, then your eliminating things that are very hard to find when troubleshooting.

You can if you have changed the power switch to better secure and insulate it, while the chassis is disassembled, test both PSU’s, Solid State Relay, adjust them if needed or replace them.

The reassembly process is similar in reverse order.

  • Complete and test any changes in PSU and cable channel
  • Install center divider and its 2 screw/nuts.
  • Install LPF its 2 flush screws and 1 screw/nut on center divider, install ribbon cable
  • Install TUNER 2 flush screws and 2 screw/nuts on center divider, install DB25 cable
  • Tilt PA module in and install 4 large screws under the chassis, install ribbon cable, SMA small coax and solder PA OUT cable from LPF, dress the cables in the plastic retainers.
  • Install the fan screws through the rear panel and the 2 flush screws on other side taking care to ensure the cables are routed so they do not bind or touch metal of the chassis or trays. Remember 240VAC on rear panel.
  • Verify on Control board, 80mm fan cable is correctly polarized, GND towards edge of board upper 3 pin header.
  • Verify on Control board, GREEN barrier strip all connections are good and tight
  • Verify the four ribbon cables are in correct headers. PA, ATU/AUX, BAND, LPF (bottom)
  • Verify Mega is seated
  • Verify both PA fans are correctly polarized, GND towards edge
  • Verify USB, HDMI and other cables are routed and are not binding on their connectors, Display board touch connector is very fragile.
  • Install front panel with 4 flush screws

That completes assembly and disassembly of the RF2K+

  • Plug in power cord
  • Power up RF2K+
  • Verify amp is operational
  • Install outer cover

Overview

This procedure details a well, tested and designed migration process that is generic to all RPI3 to RPI4 boards, and specifically to RF-KIT B26 RF2K+ as of 055.287 release.

Common Questions

Why does this work

  • Raspberry product family has very large legacy body of code dating back to first RPi1
  • They, to ensure success of the RPI4 are required by their user base to enable easy migration of legacy application or lose their market share.
  • For B26 specifically the USB to USB connection is the only connection between the Mega 2650 and the RPI3/4 and it is already fully compatible and supported.
  • Migration process is already in wide use in the RPI4 community.
  • RPI4 boards are still pin for pin compatible with all other boards from GPIO perspective.

The RPI4 uses a different USB C smart supply, must be at least 3A. 100-240VAC 50/60HZ auto-switching.

You should complete RPI3 to Buster Migration before working the plumbing that follows.

  • Disassemble the chassis, to expose PSU area
  • On the rear panel at the top there are small airflow ports, trim just one out, then run the small HDMI mini connection thru to the front and install in 2nd HDMI mini slot if you want dual displays
  • On the 40W PSU, use a Molex connector matching one on top of PSU power header, and run two wires to front of chassis.
  • On RPI4 USBC supply most will fit just under the 80MM fan, trim back plug, solder wires run to the front from the Molex connector, heat shrink to insure 240VAC is insulated.
  • Remove the RPI3 HDMI connector from display, install HDMI mini end in RPI4 slot 1, other end to HDMI on display.
  • Install USB C supply with Velcro, run its cable to RPI4 USB C power connector.
  • Install the usual USB cables.
  • Optionally make a pair of L brackets and mount the RPI4 vertically, better cooling and SD card is on the side where it’s much easier to work with.
  • Assuming you migrated your RPI3 SD to buster, remove from RPI3, install in RPI4
  • Boot system and you’re up.

What would common failures be

  1. Improperly formatted SD card.
  2. Slow SD card
  3. Corrupted B26 image file
  4. Bad RPI4 board
  5. Not following instructions
  6. Not allowing process to finish (takes 4-5 hours end to end)
  7. Bad USB to USB cable
  8. Bad power source

How to recover from migration failure

  1. Run SD formatter with sizing option ON for full expansion of partition
  2. Eject SD card to insure its finished writing
  3. Use etcher to reimage SD
  4. Re run migration process

Risks

  1. RPI4 does not boot, re run process
  2. RPI3 corrupts during Buster upgrade, follow process
  3. No hardware risk associated with this migration
  4. Raspbian is very stable, usual apt update/upgrade should not impact operation
  5. User does not follow instructions

  1. New Fast 32GB SD card
  2. Download latest image from RK-KIT FTP site
  3. RPI3 (existing card or 2nd card not in your amp as host for migration host)

16dB Attenuator Upgrade

Today’s Transceivers have the possibility of overdriving and destroying your LDMOS devices. BLR189/188XR both have a maximum -6 to +11 volt input. RFK recommends 13dB attenuator, but this is not enough to insure overdrive does not occur. A 16dB attenuator enables running to 60W ->1.5W at LDMOS input. This gives greater safety margin over the stock 13dB attenuator that came with the kit.

For 100 W input and 1.5W to the LDMOS 18.24 or 18.5dB attenuator is optional hard to find however.

A 16dB attenuator allows to 60W input safely, the setting however does not need to be changed as its already set to the trip point of 27-28W which now is 2 x or 54-56W trip point keeping LDMOS below 1.5W (-6, +11V).

In review of forum’s and literature, this is an important option in particular for iCom radios which use % instead of Watts and are well known for bursting overdrive regardless of power setting. The amp does have protection however, 60 or 100W 16 or 18.5dB attenuator reduces the risk of overdrive. At 6M remember most transceivers only put out 50W, therefore changing the attenuator will drop the 6M output somewhat.

As far as adjustments R14

On the main screen you will see a small gray/green line below the Forward Power strip display. This shows relative input drive compared to the trip point.

At your desired trip point power level 16db 60W, at 56W for example you set the bar so it shows yellow to the right end of the bar (all yellow at 56W)

  • Menu->Settings-Display 2.
  • Select Operate (GREEN)
  • Set transceiver to 54W just below 56W desired maximum in earlier step.
  • Key Transceiver CW or AM carrier 1.9Mhz
  • Adjust R14 until yellow bar just fills whole gray/green line.

You now have an indicator on the main display to alert you to your relative input drive.

Be sure to test same power level in all bands to insure both trip points and this display is correct.

TOP LEVEL ARCHITECTURE

This is a discussion of overall end to end solution, at block diagram level.

SWR BRIDGE

Many folks have asked about the B26 SWR bridge, this was a good discussion to help understand how it works.

The SWR Bridge uses a Tandem Match Sensor

A pair of 1:40 turn ferrite torid transformers are used to develop FWD and REF voltages for input to the AD8307 in previous paragraph.

NOTE: with DDM in ohms mode measuring across center and shield will give the resistance of the 40T transformer about 0.004ohms. This is normal.

PA

This section is for techincal discussion of the PA Pallet

RELAY

The relay board routes ant 1-4 to TRX port to the transceiver for RECEIVE mode. This is the default no relays active position, signal does go through TUNER and SWR BRIDGE. In the TRANSMIT mode, K2 and K3 energize, ant 1-4 is connected to through ATU, SWR BRIDGE, to LPF IN from PA OUT, at the same time K3 connect TRX to the small coax then to PA IN driving the PA Pallet from the transceiver.

K1, K4 and K5 determine which of 4 antenna ports is selected receive or transmit mode.

The Relay board also sends a TX Measurement signal back to the Control board via the RJ45 connector. That connector drives individual relays on the RELAY board via the Control board and switches TX Measurement via a capacitive voltage divider to X9 which has small coax to Control board for additional measurement.

Default is REC, Ant 1. See RELAY board in schematic sectio which has RED and GREEN TX/RX signal path flow arrows.

LPF

There are 6 Low Pass Filters, one of which is always selected (ACTIVE) for the BAND that UNIVERSAL or CAT etc. interfaces have determined. All non selected filters are shunted to ground to reduce cross coupling effects. Each filter has input and output relay pair, all inputs are connected to LPF IN, but only one is selected rest are at ground. All the filters are connected, however the same only one is selected per band. This insures no conflict between filters. The filters are: 6M, 10/12/15M, 17/20M, 30/40M, 80M, 160M.

The Control board is sent a sample from Pin 10 of the ribbon cable connector on front of the LPF that senses voltage at the LPF INPUT. (Note: no details of how the MEGA handles this input is currently available.)

The SMA connector on the Rear Panel, provides a RF sample to oscilloscope or spectrum analyzer to sample the RF output safely attenuated. This is useful for advanced testing, system monitoring or similar purposes. Always test how much voltage at legal limit is before using spectrum analyzer as they are sensitive to over voltage. Most scopes on 10X are not at risk. Therefore determin p-p, then configure your instrument to safely measure.

There is limited information on the RF sample port, what has been shared is this was done for Apache 200D and 7000DLE originally, with noticeable driver difference. To improve dynamic range a 4pf cap can be paralleled with C100 to provide additional 10db gain, a step attenuator or similar can be used to decrease signal level to acceptable limits of the device your working with. There is note in the forum that this output is -55db, (-S), that is not confirmed at this time for RF2K+. Using 7610 at 15W drive into 13db attenuator 1455W, on active antenna the RF sample port was less than 200mv p-p on Tektronix MSO 2024 100mv/div, 400ms sweep.

The 10 pin ribbon cable connector remaining pins driven by the Controller selecting filter relay pairs, provide ground and +12V.

TUNER

The TUNER is different from other designs, you tune with your transceiver keyed with AMP in standby to select the best combination of L and C values. C values range from 5, 10,20,40,80,160,320,600 pf. L values have similar progression (L value information is not yet available.)

The SWR Bridge is in series with the TUNER which is a L configuration that can switch between L or C input depending on best match that the SWR bridge and Controller select. Generally you get to 1.5:1 with AUTO mode and the more MANUEL settings STORE, with CAT etc, Amp will make adjustments as you tune the transceiver through the band, and select band with closest stored match if transceiver band is changed.

The TUNER can be BYPASSED, via pair of relays on TUNER board. On Front DISPLAY hit the TUNER menu to insert or BYPASS the TUNER. If you have external tuner, its not recommended to use amp’s TUNER, on the transceiver disable auto tuning or the amp and transceiver will conflict with each other.

When the TUNER is BYPASSED only the SWR bridge and LPF is in circuit.

In STANDBY (RED) mode TUNER is measuring Transceiver SWR, in OPERATE(GREEN), the PA OUTPUT to selected antenna is measured, and the gray/green/yellow INPUT drive display gives relative % of safe INPUT drive via the Control board. This is a RF voltage measurement not SWR specifically.

In STANDBY(RED) mode, the TUNER can be manually tuned to get SWR down to lowest value, then store this value in the persistent MEMORY Bank. See test procedure for pre setting each band to build set of frequency L/C settings stored in memory.

As the Controller selects each L/C trial setting it compares for lowest (1.5:1) until it matches, it also switches between capacitive input and inductor input L filter configurations automatically as it attemps to find acceptable SWR. If it fails high SWR message is displayed and you have to TUNER reset.
