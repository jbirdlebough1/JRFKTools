01 Prerequisites & Hardware Preparation

Before initiating the 32-bit to 64-bit migration of your RF-KIT linear amplifier, ensure you have gathered the required hardware components and verified the firmware version of your amplifier. Following these requirements guarantees complete zero-risk protection of your calibration data.

CRITICAL FIRMWARE PREREQUISITE: Your RF-KIT amplifier must run firmware version v199, v200, or later prior to starting the migration. Do not attempt migration on obsolete v198 or earlier firmware, as internal controller parameter schemas were updated in v199 to support 64-bit SimpleRPC protocol parity. Run the official RF-KIT online software update first if needed.

The 6-Card MicroSD Architecture Fleet

Professional broadcast stations and mission-critical amateur stations never rely on a single storage medium. You must procure six (6) high-endurance 32GB MicroSD cards (SanDisk Extreme, Ultra, or Pro rated V30, A2/A3, or faster with minimum 100 MB/s read and 60 MB/s write speeds) along with a reliable USB 2.0 MicroSD reader/writer.

02 The Golden Rule of Safety: Creating GOLD1 & GOLD2

The foundational principle of JRFK is zero data loss and zero risk. Before modifying any storage medium or flashing 64-bit binaries, you will clone your existing 32-bit working system into two independent physical Golden Masters directly on the Raspberry Pi.

STEP 2.1 Insert Target MicroSD into USB 2.0 Reader

Plug Card 1 into your USB 2.0 MicroSD reader and connect it to a black USB 2.0 port on the Raspberry Pi while your working 32-bit system is booted.

STEP 2.2 Launch SD Card Copier (`piclone`)

On the Raspberry Pi desktop, navigate to Application Menu (Raspberry icon) > Accessories > SD Card Copier.

  • Copy From Device: Select Internal SD card (/dev/mmcblk0).
  • Copy To Device: Select your USB 2.0 reader, e.g. Mass Storage Device (/dev/sda).
  • New Partition UUIDs: Leave unchecked (this ensures an exact identical clone).
  • Click Start. When the prompt asks for confirmation to wipe the USB card, click OK.

STEP 2.3 Label and Repeat for GOLD2

When the cloning process completes (typically 4–8 minutes), remove the card and immediately attach the physical adhesive label: GOLD1. Next, insert Card 2 and repeat the identical process to create GOLD2.

THE ZERO-RISK GUARANTEE: With GOLD1 and GOLD2 safely in hand, you have complete peace of mind. Both cards contain your exact 32-bit operating system, custom screen calibration, Wi-Fi credentials, and amplifier tuning tables. If at any time you wish to revert, simply insert either GOLD card back into the Raspberry Pi and power on.

STEP 3.1 Launch JRFK.exe on Your PC

Run JRFK.exe on your Windows workstation. JRFK starts its local administrative engine and opens the migration console in Google Chrome.

STEP 3.2 Insert GOLD1 into PC USB 2.0 Reader

Plug GOLD1 into your PC's USB 2.0 reader. JRFK immediately identifies the volume in strictly read-only mode. It extracts and archives:

  • /home/pi/rf-kit-statics/config.json (Station callsign, antenna port mappings, custom names)
  • Calibration lookup tables (SWR null, forward/reflected detector polynomials)
  • Antenna Tuner (ATU) band segment matrices (L and C matching values)
  • Network configuration (/etc/wpa_supplicant/wpa_supplicant.conf)
  • Touchscreen alignment parameters (/etc/pointercal and Xorg evdev configs)
HARDWARE WRITE-BLOCKING: JRFK will strictly refuse to write to or format any disk identified as a Golden Master. Once extraction completes, the application instructs you to remove the GOLD card and set it safely aside.

STEP 3.3 Insert Target Card 3 (PROD-64) & Execute Clean Wipe

Insert your blank Card 3 (PROD-64) into the USB 2.0 reader. JRFK performs strict safety checks:

  1. Verifies the drive is Removable (blocking system drive C:, D:, and fixed NVMe/SSDs).
  2. Confirms capacity is <= 64 GB.
  3. Executes a low-level DISKPART clean operation to purge corrupt partition tables.

STEP 3.4 Flash 64-Bit OS & Inject JRFK Payload

Click Flash 64-Bit OS & Inject JRFK Payload. The migrator automatically:

  • Flashes the official 64-bit Debian Trixie (aarch64) Raspberry Pi OS image.
  • Injects the complete offline package archive into the boot partition (including 15 arm64 .deb packages, fonts-lato, libhamlib, python3-legacy-cgi, and pure-Python simple_rpc).
  • Configures firstboot_setup.sh to run unattended on the amplifier.
  • Sets 1024x600 display resolution timings in cmdline.txt and config.txt.

06 RFK Diagnostic Suite Operator's Guide (Port 8088)

The JRFK 64-bit environment includes a standalone diagnostic and disaster recovery web suite operating on Port 8088 (e.g., http://192.168.12.193:8088). Designed in high-contrast Medium Blueprint Format (crisp white typography and white grid on medium cyanotype blue #1a5695), it provides comprehensive real-time telemetry and safety controls.

6.1 Antenna Mapper & Tuner Bode Plot (`/antenna`)

The Antenna Mapper renders the live SWR characterization curve for each band segment, syncs directly with the RF-KIT antenna port map, and reports true physical component values.

ANTENNA MAPPER SPECIFICATION & BODE MARKERS
• Segment Boundaries (|): The Bode plot displays vertical dashed dividing lines with a "|" marker at each segment boundary (e.g., 25 kHz segments on 20m: 14.000, 14.025, 14.050 ... 14.350 MHz).
• Antenna Port Map Synchronization: The "ACTIVE ANTENNA" indicator dynamically reflects the selected band's antenna port assignment from config.json (e.g., Band 20m → "ANT 2: SteppIR 20m-6m"). As you change bands via the spinner or buttons, the antenna field and Bode plot update in lockstep.
• Physical Component Values (No Raw Clicks!): Instead of obscure stepper clicks, the inspector reports true engineering units: - Inductance (L): Displayed in nanohenries (nH) and microhenries (µH) (e.g., 1,750 nH / 1.75 µH). - Capacitance (C): Displayed in picofarads (pF) (e.g., 385 pF). - Matching Topology: Indicates CL (Cin / Tx Side) or LC (Cout / Ant Side) relay configuration.

6.2 Amplifier Health Monitor & 3-Tier Thermal Guard (`/health`)

The Health Monitor provides real-time oversight of the PA heatsink temperature, 53.5V DC rail stability, drain current, and RF power envelope. It features an automated hardware-protective Thermal Guard State Machine:

Thermal Tier Heatsink Range Color Indicator System Action & Safety Protocol
Tier 1: Normal <= 50.0°C GREEN Standard operating duty. Cooling fans operate automatically (Fan On > 42°C, Fan Off < 38°C).
Tier 2: Warning 50.1°C - 55.0°C YELLOW Warning Banner Displayed. Telemetry line turns yellow. High thermal load alert generated.
Tier 3: Alert 55.1°C - 60.0°C RED Critical Alert Banner. Telemetry line turns red. Operator warned of imminent safety shutdown.
Tier 4: Trip Limit > 60.0°C UNKEY TRIP EMERGENCY UNKEY & PROTECTION LOCKOUT:
1. Instantly unkeys amplifier (switches core to Standby).
2. Displays full-screen modal alert: "AMPLIFIER DISABLED AND PROTECTED".
3. Initiates live 300-second (5 minute) countdown timer.
4. Interlock: Re-enables amplifier ONLY when timer reaches 0 AND heatsink temp cools below 50.0°C.

6.3 Circular Telemetry Logging (10 MB Rotating Buffer)

To prevent storage exhaustion and SD card write wear, JRFK implements a strict 10MB rotating circular log located at /home/pi/rfk_tools/telemetry_rolling.log. The logging engine computes rolling statistical summaries:

  • PA Temperature (°C): Current, Typical (running average), Minimum, and Maximum.
  • DC Rail Voltage (V): Current, Typ, Min, and Max (monitoring 53.5V regulation under heavy CW/SSB key-down).
  • DC Drain Current (A): Current, Typ, Min, and Max (up to 35A under full 1.5kW carrier).
  • Forward & Reflected Power (W): Current, Typ, Min, Max, and calculated SWR.

6.4 Remote Web Screen Viewer (`/screen`)

Navigating to http://<amplifier-ip>:8088/screen opens a high-fidelity live stream of the console display with configurable refresh rates (1.0s, 2.0s, 5.0s, or Pause) and full-screen presentation mode.

6.7 Automated SD Backup & Disaster Recovery (`/backup`)

By permanently leaving Card 5 (BACKUP-AUTO) inside the amplifier's external USB 2.0 reader, the system performs unattended weekly whole-disk cloning via /etc/cron.d/jrfk_weekly_backup or instant on-demand replication via the web interface. If the internal production SD ever degrades or fails, simply power down, move Card 5 into the internal Raspberry Pi slot, and power back on—your amplifier is immediately back online with all settings intact.

6-STAGE REPLICATION PIPELINE & MANDATORY PRE-SUCCESS VERIFICATION
The JRFK Disaster Recovery Engine strictly prohibits declaring success until a rigorous 5-point post-replication integrity audit completes without errors:

• Stage 1 (Unmount): Unmounts all active volumes on the target USB 2.0 SD device (/dev/sda).
• Stage 2 (Partition): Writes a standard DOS partition table (512 MB FAT32 Boot + remaining storage EXT4 Rootfs).
• Stage 3 (Format): Formats pristine target filesystems via mkfs.vfat -F 32 and mkfs.ext4.
• Stage 4 (Replication): Synchronizes bootloader firmware and 64-bit rootfs via differential block rsync (replicating ~6.8 GB of used data in 3–4 minutes, excluding volatile runtimes and swap).
• Stage 5 (UUID Patching): Extracts unique hardware PARTUUIDs from the new SD partitions and automatically updates target cmdline.txt and /etc/fstab so the card boots independently with zero dependency on the source.
• Stage 6 (Mandatory Verification Suite):
  1. Bootloader & Kernel Check: Verifies existence, permissions, and byte length of cmdline.txt, config.txt, and kernel8.img.
  2. PARTUUID Alignment Check: Validates that both cmdline.txt and /etc/fstab target the destination card's PARTUUID.
  3. OS Binary Tree Check: Confirms critical root directory structures (/bin, /sbin, /usr, /etc, /lib, /var) and core binaries.
  4. Station Calibration & Tuning Audit: Compares /home/pi/PA-Files, antenna matrices, null voltage calibrations, and diagnostic suite files against the active amplifier with 100% parity verification.
  5. Cache Flush & Non-Destructive Filesystem Check: Executes hardware buffer flush (sync), unmounts partitions, and runs non-destructive read-only checks (fsck.vfat -n and e2fsck -nf) confirming 0 filesystem errors.
