Why Legacy 32-Bit Raspberry Pi OS Must Go

RISK 1: UPSTREAM SOFTWARE END-OF-LIFE
⚠️ Unmaintained 32-Bit Linux Kernel
Upstream Debian and Raspberry Pi OS have deprecated 32-bit architectures. Critical security fixes, controller driver patches, and repository dependencies are completely frozen, leaving aging installations open to software regressions without vendor support.
RISK 2: FLASH MEDIA FATIGUE & CRASHES
💾 Unbuffered SD Card Corruption
Legacy 32-bit deployments lack wear-leveling monitoring, read-only flash overlays, and automated backup hooks. A sudden station power drop or dirty shutdown during amplifier operation risks permanent corruption of the master filesystem and factory calibration matrices.
RISK 3: SERIAL COMMUNICATIONS JITTER
⚡ SimpleRPC Socket Timeouts & Drift
Legacy 32-bit userland stacks suffer from socket starvation and thread priority jitter on the controller interface. Under high RF environments or heavy CAT decoding loads, communication lockups can blind the GUI to instantaneous amplifier telemetry.
RISK 4: AMPLIFIER SAFETY LOCKS
🛑 Inability to Run Concurrent Supervision
32-bit OS constraints prevent running simultaneous remote telemetry daemons alongside the front panel application. Shack operators have zero independent browser-based access to the 60°C thermal trips, reflected power alarms, or live antenna matrix while operating.
